# What Google keeps of content sent to the Gemini API, and under which terms

Research for EXP-63 (map EXP-52). It feeds the DPIA; it is **not** the person-facing disclosure.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a factual reading of Google's published terms and docs as of the
> fetch date. It records what the pages say and marks inference separately. Contract interpretation
> (e.g. whether a clause binds the free tier in the EEA) is for counsel.

- **Fetched:** 2026-09-27, all pages below, via plain HTTP GET (no account, no sign-in).
- **Context:** ExpertToJob sends career narratives to the Gemini API **embeddings** endpoint
  (`api/Mcp/appsettings.json`: `"EmbeddingModel": "gemini-embedding-001"`, via
  `generativelanguage.googleapis.com` `embedContent`). That is the Gemini Developer API, **not**
  Vertex AI.

## What counts as paid vs unpaid

S1: "Any Services that are offered free of charge like direct interactions with Google AI Studio or
unpaid quota in Gemini API are unpaid Services (the "Unpaid Services")."

S1: "Your access to Gemini API is a "Paid Service" only when accessing the API through a Cloud
Project associated with an active billing account."

S1 (Use Restrictions): "You may use only Paid Services when making API Clients available to users in
the European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom."

## 1. Paid services — what is logged, how long, why, human review

**Terms say (S1):**

> "When you use Paid Services, including, for example, the paid quota of the Gemini API, Google
> doesn't use your prompts (including associated system instructions, cached content, and files such
> as images, videos, or documents) or responses to improve our products, and will process your
> prompts and responses in accordance with the Data Processing Addendum for Products Where Google is
> a Data Processor. For Paid Services, Google logs prompts and responses for a limited period of
> time, solely for detecting and preventing violations of the Prohibited Use Policy to maintain the
> safety and security of the Services, and any required legal or regulatory disclosures. This data
> may be stored transiently or cached in any country in which Google or its agents maintain
> facilities."

> "Other data we collect while providing the Paid Services to you, such as account information and
> settings, billing history, direct communications and feedback, and usage details (e.g., ...token
> count per prompt and response, operational status, safety filter triggers, ... IP addresses)
> remains subject to the Google Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms and Google Privacy Policy
> referenced in the API Terms."

**Duration and human review (S2, Abuse monitoring):**

> "Google retains the following data for fifty-five (55) days for the purposes of detecting and
> preventing violations of the Prohibited Use Policy to maintain the safety and security of the
> Services, and any required legal or regulatory disclosures: Prompts ... Contextual Information ...
> Output: The responses generated by the Gemini API."

> "Automated Detection: Automated systems scan API usage for violations of our Prohibited Use
> Policy ... Manual Detection: If a project consistently exhibits suspicious activity, it may be
> flagged for manual review by authorized Google personnel."

> "When prompts or model outputs are flagged by safety filters and abuse detection systems described
> above, authorized Google employees may assess the flagged content ... When data is logged for abuse
> monitoring, it is used solely for the purpose of policy enforcement and preventing policy
> violations. It is not used to train or fine-tune any AI/ML models besides those used specifically
> for policy enforcement."

S2 "Scope": "These policy guidelines apply to the use of the Gemini API and AI Studio." (Not
limited to paid; the page does not distinguish tiers.)

**No opt-out on the Developer API (S5):**

> "Prompt logging for abuse monitoring: ... for Paid Services, Google logs prompts and responses for
> a limited period of time solely for detecting violations of the Prohibited Use Policy. If your
> workload requires guaranteed zero data retention or enterprise data processing agreements, use
> Vertex AI."

**Separate, opt-in developer logs (S3, S4)** — distinct from abuse logs ("These logs which are
private to your Google Cloud project are separate from any logs held solely for abuse monitoring
purposes"). Default max 55 days, configurable to 7/14/28/55; only if the project owner enables
logging; sharing a dataset with Google switches it to Unpaid-Services data use.

**Inference:** for a billed project, a career narrative sent to Gemini is kept up to 55 days in
abuse-monitoring logs, is not used for training/product improvement, may be seen by authorised
Google staff if flagged, and may be stored "in any country". There is no customer control on the
Developer API to switch the abuse log off.

## 2. Unpaid services, operator outside EEA/UK/CH

**Terms say (S1):**

> "When you use Unpaid Services, including, for example, Google AI Studio and the unpaid quota on
> Gemini API, Google uses the content you submit to the Services and any generated responses to
> provide, improve, and develop Google products and services and machine learning technologies,
> including Google's enterprise features, products, and services, consistent with our Privacy
> Policy."

> "To help with quality and improve our products, human reviewers may read, annotate, and process
> your API input and output. Google takes steps to protect your privacy as part of this process.
> This includes disconnecting this data from your Google Account, API key, and Cloud project before
> reviewers see or annotate it. Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to
> the Unpaid Services."

Plus the API Terms licence (S9 §b): "you give Google a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide,
sublicensable, royalty-free, and non-exclusive license to Use content submitted ... "Use" means use,
host, store, modify, communicate, and publish." S1 extends it "to any content (e.g., prompts ...)
you submit to the Services and to any generated responses."

S10 pricing, per model including **Gemini Embedding 2**: "Used to improve our products — Free Tier:
Yes / Paid Tier: No".

**Retention period: not stated.** Neither S1 nor S3/S5 gives a retention period for free-tier
content used for improvement. S2's 55-day figure is expressly for abuse-monitoring data.

**Inference:** free-tier content, once used for improvement/training, has no published end date;
the perpetual licence points the same way. S1 itself tells developers not to send personal
information to Unpaid Services — career narratives are personal data, so the free tier is
unsuitable for them regardless of region.

## 3. Unpaid services, operator in EEA/UK/CH

**Terms say (S1):**

> "If you're in the European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom, the terms under "How
> Google uses Your Data" in "Paid Services" apply to all Services, including Google AI Studio and
> unpaid quota in the Gemini API, even though they are offered free of charge."

So the terms that apply are exactly the paid "How Google Uses Your Data" subsection quoted in §1:
no product-improvement use; processing "in accordance with the Data Processing Addendum"; logging
"for a limited period of time, solely for" Prohibited-Use-Policy enforcement and legal disclosures;
transient storage/caching "in any country"; and the usage-data/account-data carve-out to the
Controller-Controller terms.

Separately, S1: "You may use only Paid Services when making API Clients available to users in the
European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom." S11 FAQ: "Can I use the Gemini API for
free in EEA (including EU), the UK, and CH? Yes, we make the free tier and paid tier available in
many regions."

**Not settled by the text (inference / open points):**

- The EEA carve-out imports the paid *data-use subsection*, not paid status. The DPA's scope (S7)
  lists the processor service as **"Gemini API Paid Services — Personal data processed by Gemini
  API Paid Services in submitted prompts and responses to those prompts."** Whether the DPA thereby
  binds an EEA operator's free-tier traffic is not stated; S1's subsection says Google "will
  process ... in accordance with" the DPA, which suggests yes, but S7 names only Paid Services.
- The contracting entity for Paid Services (S8) is keyed to "Gemini API Paid Services"; for unpaid
  use S9 makes "Google" = Google LLC (see §6).
- ExpertToJob's product makes an API Client available to users (Service Managers), so for EEA users
  S1's "only Paid Services" restriction applies anyway. Using a billed project is the reading that
  avoids all three ambiguities.

## 4. Embeddings specifically

**Terms say:** S1 does **not** mention embeddings, `embedContent`, or any endpoint. Its data terms
are framed on "content you submit", "prompts" and "responses". S2 and S5 likewise say "prompts" and
"responses"/"Output" and do not list endpoints. No page gives a different retention period for
embeddings.

Where embeddings *are* named:

- S10 pricing lists **gemini-embedding-2** with the same "Used to improve our products: Free Yes /
  Paid No" row as generation models. `gemini-embedding-001` (the model the repo uses) does not
  appear on the pricing page at fetch time; S12 says "For text-only use cases, gemini-embedding-001
  remains available."
- S4 (developer-owned logs): "Logging is not currently supported for the following: ... Gemini
  embedding models". Supported calls are "GenerateContent, BatchGenerateContent,
  StreamGenerateContent API calls, and Interactions API calls". This is about the **opt-in project
  logs**, not the abuse-monitoring logs.

**Inference:** embeddings are part of "the Services" / "Gemini API" and so fall under the same
Additional Terms as generation — no carve-out exists in either direction. The terms do not say
whether abuse-monitoring logging (S2) is actually applied to `embedContent` calls; S2 and S1 read on
their face as covering all prompts to the API, so the DPIA should assume the 55-day abuse log
applies to embedding inputs. The embedding vector itself is the "response". Developer-side logging
is unavailable for embeddings, so the opt-in 7–55-day project log does not add a copy.

## 5. Deletion and the DPA / controller–processor arrangement

**DPA exists for the Gemini API (not only Vertex AI).** S1 (Paid Services) links the "Data
Processing Addendum for Products Where Google is a Data Processor" (S6), and S7 lists "Gemini API
Paid Services" among the "Data Processing Services" subject to it, with personal data types
"Personal data processed by Gemini API Paid Services in submitted prompts and responses to those
prompts." Account, billing and usage data are instead under the Controller-Controller terms (S1
quote in §1). So: Google = processor for prompt/response content on Paid Services; Google =
independent controller for account/usage telemetry.

**Deletion terms (S6):**

> "6.1.2 Processor Services Without Deletion Functionality. During the Term, if the functionality of
> the Processor Services does not include the option for Partner to delete Partner Personal Data,
> then Google will comply with: (a) any reasonable request from Partner to facilitate such deletion,
> insofar as this is possible taking into account the nature and functionality of the Processor
> Services and unless applicable laws require storage; and (b) Google may charge a fee (based on
> Google's reasonable costs) for any data deletion under Section 6.1.2 (a)."

> "6.2 Deletion on Term Expiry. Partner instructs Google to delete all remaining Partner Personal
> Data (including existing copies) from Google's systems at the end of the Term in accordance with
> applicable law."

(6.1.1 covers services that do offer self-service deletion.)

**What the Gemini docs offer for deletion:** the File API ("Files are stored at-rest until deleted
by the user or until they expire"), cached content (user-set TTL), and tuned models (S1: "When you
delete a tuned model, the related tuning content is also deleted"). None applies to an `embedContent`
call. No Gemini page describes a self-service way to delete abuse-monitoring logs; S5 points to
Vertex AI for "guaranteed zero data retention or enterprise data processing agreements".

**Inference:** for embedding calls there is no deletion function, so S6 §6.1.2 applies — a
"reasonable request", best-effort, possibly for a fee — backstopped by the 55-day abuse-log expiry.
Free-tier content outside the EEA has no DPA and no deletion promise in the terms.

**Vertex AI contrast (stated only in S5):** ZDR and "enterprise data processing agreements" are
offered via Vertex AI (Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform); the Developer API offers neither.

## 6. Counterparty for an EEA operator and transfer mechanism

**Paid Services — S1:** "For Paid Services, "Google" as used in these Terms has the meaning given
here" → S8, whose first table covers "Google Cloud Platform, Google Cloud Marketplace, **Gemini API
Paid Services**, ...". By customer billing address:

| Billing address | Google contracting entity |
|---|---|
| EMEA except France, Italy and Poland | Google Cloud EMEA Limited |
| France | Google Cloud France |
| Italy | Google Cloud Italy S.r.l. |
| Poland | Google Cloud Poland Sp. z o.o |
| United States and any other location not covered above | Google LLC |

S6: "“Google” means the Google Entity that is party to the Agreement." So the DPA counterparty is the
same entity.

**Unpaid Services — S9:** "Under the Terms, "Google" means Google LLC, with offices at 1600
Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, California 94043, United States, unless set forth otherwise in
additional terms applicable for a given API." S1 only sets the entity otherwise "For Paid Services".

**Transfer mechanism (S6, Appendix 3A):**

> "7.1 Restricted European Transfers. ... (a) The parties acknowledge that Google has adopted a Data
> Transfer Solution for any Restricted European Transfer, and the parties will ensure that such
> Restricted European Transfer is made in accordance with that solution; and/or (b) if a Data
> Transfer Solution is not available, then: ... (A) the SCCs (Processor-to-Processor, Google
> Exporter) will apply with respect to such Restricted European Transfers from Google to
> Subprocessors; and (B) in addition, if Partner's address is not in an Adequate Country, the SCCs
> (Processor-to-Controller) will apply ... or (ii) if Google's address is not in an Adequate
> Country, the SCCs (Controller-to-Processor) and/or SCCs (Processor-to-Processor) will apply".

> "7.4 ... The parties acknowledge that Google has certified under the Data Privacy Framework on
> behalf of itself and certain wholly-owned US subsidiaries. ... The Data Privacy Framework will
> apply to any Restricted European Transfer to a certified Google entity in the US."

S6 §10.1: "Google may process Partner Personal Data in any country in which Google or its
Subprocessors maintain facilities." S1 likewise: prompt/response data "may be stored transiently or
cached in any country in which Google or its agents maintain facilities."

**Inference:** an EEA operator (outside FR/IT/PL) on a billed project contracts with **Google Cloud
EMEA Limited** (Ireland); onward transfers to the US rely primarily on the EU–US Data Privacy
Framework, with Google's intra-group P2P SCCs as fallback. No data-residency commitment exists for
the Developer API.
